## Scope and evidence

I compared the practice-depth brief and current canonical 316-question source to author A’s D01/D02 notes, author B’s D03/D04 matrix and retained 12 full objects, and author C’s D05/D06/D07 coverage matrix. The current curriculum objective labels in the draft unit registry were treated as the scope boundary. Author drafts are private candidates and not canonical content.

The combined visible retained additions are 14 from A, 12 from B, and 45 from C: at most 387 total questions against the current 316 baseline if every candidate were accepted. This remains well below the user’s explicit expectation of more than 840. That is not a reason to pad or apply a per-unit quota; it means these reviews must remain intermediate and authors must continue meaningful practice expansion after correcting the concrete item defects below.

## Smallest coherent coverage-led method

Maintain a short ledger per learning objective, organized by consequential **decision family**, not by broad mechanism. For each family record: (1) canonical peer IDs; (2) the decisive facts that trigger the accepted action; (3) a changed-fact counterfactual that should trigger the closest alternative; (4) a plausible novice error and its concrete consequence; and (5) evidence/workload forms relevant to that objective (for example a trace, numeric budget, source-version conflict, approval state, or rare/high-impact slice). Mark which cases give learners actual practice on each side.

Add a case when it makes learners apply the same rule under a changed condition, diagnose a different plausible error, or interpret materially different evidence. A pair that reverses the decision is particularly useful for reducing answer memorization. Do not demand that every case have a matched pair, that every family use every evidence form, or that each unit hit a number. Conversely, do not close a unit merely because each mechanism appears once. Stop adding to a unit only when its consequential families have repeated, varied application of the core rule, relevant near-miss errors are diagnosed, and additional proposed stems would fail to change the reasoning or learning value. Keep blueprint alignment explicitly unresolved until the actual Professional blueprint is available.

For each candidate, reviewers should read the complete prompt, options, key, reason/details, every option-targeted feedback message, and its source refs; independently defend the key against the nearest distractor; then compare against all baseline peers and cross-unit peers. Contract validation and score tests are necessary but do not establish any of these semantic conditions.

## Design scores

- Objective/architecture fit: **0.92** — variable, objective-led expansion and disjoint private authoring preserve current source contracts; the user’s >840 objective is not yet reached by this candidate tranche.
- Simplicity: **0.90** — one decision-family ledger and canonical JSON additions are a proportionate authoring workflow; avoid turning the ledger into a second approval or curriculum system.
- Risk: **0.80** — safe on schema/runtime/historical state, but current source-mismatch and wrong-feedback examples show why per-item semantic review is mandatory; the modest margin means do not weaken checks.
- Maintainability: **0.88** — baseline peer IDs, changed decisive facts and versioned private outputs are maintainable if the ledger remains concise and is consolidated into the single current evidence record.
- Minimum: **0.80** — proceed with the method, while correcting concrete item defects and continuing coverage-led authoring; redesign if the workflow begins using “no new mechanism” as its closure test.
